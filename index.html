Profile

I work where logic, formal verification and AI meet, with a focus on trustworthy AI that keeps a human in the loop.

I co-created spatial model checking for image analysis, a research line now in its tenth year, and have worked on all of it: metatheory (completeness, bisimilarity, minimisation), language design, high-performance execution (GPU kernels, lazy parallel evaluation, multi-level caching) and hybrid symbolic–neural AI, applied to medical imaging (brain tumours, acute ischemic stroke).

I work daily with frontier AI models and agents under formally grounded supervision, a method I turned into a tool, NAIMA. An Anthropic AI for Science Program award (2026) funds an ongoing experiment in which LLM agents produce new results in explainable, symbolic medical image segmentation.

About 1,700 citations and an h-index of 20 (Google Scholar, October 2026); our spatial logics and model checkers are cited by research groups in 16 countries.

Open problems I’m working on

Explainable, symbolic image segmentation as accurate as neural networks

The problem
Can segmentation procedures written as readable logical specifications match the accuracy of end-to-end neural networks on medical images?
Why it is hard
Hand-written symbolic procedures have long trailed neural networks in accuracy, and the space of candidate programs is large; every improvement must keep the procedure inspectable and justified, not just better-scoring.
What I am doing
An LLM-agent research loop that I direct proposes, runs and refines segmentation programs in the declarative language of VoxLogicA 2, each with a stated rationale and versioned for reproducibility, on public brain-tumour benchmarks. This is hybrid AI with a strong symbolic component; it is funded by the Anthropic AI for Science Program (2026).

Choosing parameters without an oracle

The problem
How should the thresholds and parameters of a symbolic segmentation procedure be chosen when the ground truth of the image at hand is not available?
Why it is hard
Accuracy reported with parameters tuned against ground truth is an upper bound, not a deployable result. Label-free selection criteria must be justified and must not leak information from the evaluation set.
What I am doing
I keep oracle-tuned and label-free results separate in every experiment, and I am studying selection criteria that depend only on the image and on properties stated in the logic.

Medical AI workflows that are compliant by construction

The problem
Can the transparency, traceability and human-oversight requirements of the EU AI Act be part of an executable specification of a clinical AI workflow, rather than documentation written afterwards?
Why it is hard
Regulatory requirements are stated in legal language; workflows mix neural components, symbolic reasoning and human decisions, and the guarantees must hold for every execution.
What I am doing
A workflow language in which human assessment is a primitive and clinical protocols are encoded directly, so that compliance properties follow from the structure of the workflow (ISoLA 2026, to appear).

ISoLA 2026 (to appear)

LLM agents guided by formal methods

The problem
How can large amounts of software produced by LLM agents be trusted when no human can read all of it?
Why it is hard
Agents produce code faster than it can be reviewed; trust has to come from specifications, tests and process discipline that the agents themselves cannot bypass.
What I am doing
NAIMA, a fully automated, LLM-driven, parallel multi-agent project-management tool that applies established software-engineering practice and manages its own development. VoxLogicA 2 was written entirely by AI agents from a human-authored reference implementation used as an executable specification. Two publications in preparation.

NAIMA

A formally verified, high-performance spatial model checker

The problem
Can an image-analysis engine that runs unattended for weeks be both fast and correct by construction?
Why it is hard
Lazy parallel evaluation, caching and GPU kernels are where performance comes from and also where errors hide; verification and performance engineering usually pull in opposite directions.
What I am doing
VoxLogicA 2.1, built exclusively through NAIMA: its core is a formal model with automatically proved properties, from which high-performance implementations are derived.
